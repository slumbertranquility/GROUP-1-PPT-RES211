Rationale of the Study Chapter 1 · § 1
The Context

Why This matters.

In the United States, 40% of full-time undergraduate students were employed as of 2020, while 74% of part-time undergraduates worked (NCES, 2022). Across Europe, an average of 59% of students hold jobs during the lecture period.

In the Philippines, a 2022 CHED survey found that approximately 260,000 students (~8%) of the total college population work while studying.

The researchers observed an increasing number of working undergraduates in the College of Maritime Education at UCLM. Previous studies indicate that working students often experience physical exhaustion, inadequate sleep, and fatigue (Balacuit & Lopio, 2022).

Rationale · Research Gap Chapter 1 · § 1
The Gap

A localized investigation.

Observed Gap

Limited Maritime-Specific Research

Despite the availability of international and local studies on working students, limited research has specifically examined the effects of part-time employment on the academic performance of maritime students, particularly within the context of the University of Cebu Lapu-Lapu and Mandaue.

This gap in the literature highlights the need for a focused investigation of how maritime students balance academic and work responsibilities.

Study Aim

Focused Examination at UCLM

Given the growing number of maritime students engaged in part-time employment, this study aims to examine how working while studying affects their academic performance at UCLM.

Specifically, it seeks to determine the effects of part-time employment on students' grades, class attendance, study habits, and class participation, and to identify the challenges they encounter and the strategies they use to balance academic and work responsibilities.

Theoretical Background Chapter 1 · § 2
Three-Theory Foundation

Framework triad.

This study draws on three theoretical perspectives that explain how part-time employment may influence the academic performance of maritime students at UCLM — economic, sociological, and educational lenses.

Allocation of Time
Becker (1965)

Individuals allocate their limited 24-hour time resource among various activities such as work, education, and leisure. Time spent in part-time work reduces the time available for studying, resting, and completing academic tasks.

Role Strain
Goode (1960)

Focuses on psychological and social pressures that arise when individuals simultaneously occupy multiple roles. Role strain occurs when a person lacks sufficient time, energy, or emotional resources to meet role expectations.

Student Integration
Tinto (1975)

Students' academic success depends largely on their level of academic and social integration within the institution. Substantial time in part-time employment may decrease academic involvement and peer interaction.

Review of Related Literature Chapter 1 · § 3
RRL

Global perspectives.

Becker (1965) · Time Allocation
Explains how individuals distribute limited time among competing activities. An increase in time devoted to employment may reduce time for studying and completing school requirements.
NCES (2022) · Global Employment
40% of full-time and 74% of part-time undergraduates in the U.S. are employed while pursuing studies — balancing academics and employment is a common reality.
Kiss et al. (2024) · Skills Development
Part-time work can help students develop self-discipline, problem-solving skills, independence, and financial stability, which may positively influence academic performance when managed effectively.
Nguyen & Hart (2024) · Meta-Analysis
While the overall negative academic impact of part-time work is generally small, it becomes more pronounced among students who work longer hours or engage in physically demanding jobs.
Ramos & Villanueva (2025) · Time Management
Part-time employment is a "double-edged sword" — students with strong time-management skills can maintain performance, while those who struggle experience stress, fatigue, and academic decline.
Cole & Fernandez (2024) · Systematic Review
Reports mixed findings — the impact of part-time employment is context-dependent and influenced by institutional support, course difficulty, socioeconomic status, and individual resilience.

Review of Related Studies Chapter 1 · § 4
RRS · Empirical Studies

Field evidence.

✦
Alj Bory (2021)
Many working students maintained strong academic performance despite holding part-time jobs, especially when working moderate hours in supportive work environments.
Ahmed, Mohamed & Elhassan (2024)
Nearly one-fourth of medical students at Alzaiem Alazhari University engaged in part-time work; findings suggest similar outcomes may apply to maritime students due to comparable academic intensity.
Singh & Kumari (2025)
Students who worked more than 20 hours per week reported reduced study time, increased fatigue, and higher stress levels — number of work hours is a critical predictor of performance.
Irfan & Farooq (2023)
Moderate employment (fewer than 15 hours per week) was associated with better academic performance and encouraged students to be more organized and productive.
Dizon (2025) · PH Context
Financial constraints were among the primary reasons Filipino college students engaged in part-time employment — highly relevant to maritime students financing their education.

Research Questions

The problem.

This study aimed to determine the effect of part-time employment on the academic performance of maritime students at UCLM. Specifically, this study answered the following questions:

1
What is the profile of the respondents in terms of:
1.1 Age
1.2 Gender
1.3 Academic Program
1.4 Type of Job

What is the level of effect of the part-time job of the respondents on their academic performance in the aspect of:
2.1 Study habits
2.2 Timely submission of academic requirements
2.3 Attendance and class participation
2.4 Overall academic progress
3
Is there a significant relation between the profile and the level of effect of their part-time job?

Based on the study's findings, what recommendations may be proposed?

Scope and Delimitation Chapter 1 · § 7
Research Boundaries

Within the frame.

✓ WITHIN THE STUDY

Focused solely on Bachelor of Science in Marine Transportation (BSMT) students enrolled at UCLM.

Examined how part-time employment affected academic performance, grades, attendance, study habits, time management, and overall academic engagement.

Respondents included BSMT students currently engaged in part-time employment during the academic year covered by the study.

Data gathered through a survey questionnaire assessing how balancing work and academic responsibilities influenced performance.

✕ OUTSIDE THE STUDY

Did not include maritime students from other campuses or universities — findings may not be generalizable to all Filipino maritime students.

Relied on self-reported data, which may have been subject to response bias or inaccuracies.

Did not examine long-term academic outcomes beyond the covered academic year, nor did it include students engaged in full-time employment.

Factors such as family responsibilities and personal motivation were not explored unless they directly related to part-time employment.

Definition of Terms Chapter 1 · § 8
Glossary

Key terms.

Note: The Definition of Terms section appears in the Table of Contents (p. 18), but the full text was not included in the reviewed document. The following operational terms are drawn from concepts consistently referenced throughout Chapter 1.

Part-Time Employment
Work engagement of BSMT students during the academic year that occupies fewer hours than full-time employment, undertaken to cover tuition, daily expenses, and allowances.
Academic Performance
The measurable output of BSMT students in terms of grades, attendance, study habits, class participation, and overall academic progress.
Study Habits
Consistent behaviors and practices employed by students in reviewing lessons, completing assignments, and preparing for maritime coursework.
Class Participation
Active engagement of BSMT students in classroom discussions, practical exercises, and collaborative maritime learning activities.
BSMT Student
A student enrolled in the Bachelor of Science in Marine Transportation program at the University of Cebu Lapu-Lapu and Mandaue during the academic year of the study.
Work-Study Balance
The ability of a student to manage academic responsibilities alongside part-time work commitments without significantly compromising either domain.
